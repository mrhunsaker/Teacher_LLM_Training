# Practical Competency Checklist

Use this checklist during practice, peer review, coaching, or a capstone demonstration. It is designed to assess observable educator behaviors, not speed, technical vocabulary, or the amount of AI used.

## Rating scale

- **0 — Not demonstrated:** behavior is absent or creates a material risk.
- **1 — Emerging:** behavior appears with prompting or is incomplete.
- **2 — Demonstrated:** behavior is independently and consistently shown in this task.
- **N/O — Not observed:** there was no opportunity to assess this behavior.

Do not treat N/O as zero. Record a brief example or evidence for each rating.

## Competency checklist

### 1. Bounded prompt design

- [ ] States one specific task and intended audience.
- [ ] Provides only relevant, approved context.
- [ ] Defines constraints and prohibited inferences or decisions.
- [ ] Specifies an accessible output format.
- [ ] Includes a clear stop condition and asks before moving to another task.

### 2. Privacy and responsible use

- [ ] Uses fictional data or confirms the specific tool and data use are district-approved.
- [ ] Minimizes information and considers re-identification risk beyond removing names.
- [ ] Avoids unnecessary identifiers, images, schedules, and sensitive details.
- [ ] Follows local storage, sharing, retention, and reporting requirements.

### 3. Student-centered analysis

- [ ] Separates supplied/observed facts, hypotheses, and unknowns.
- [ ] Does not infer functional vision, literacy medium, independence, or need from diagnosis, acuity, grades, or device ownership alone.
- [ ] Identifies questions for the learner and relevant team members.
- [ ] Considers environment, materials, access barriers, preferences, and task purpose.

### 4. Goal and measurement quality

- [ ] Defines observable behavior and meaningful conditions.
- [ ] Defines valid opportunities and denominator.
- [ ] Distinguishes independent from prompted performance with operational definitions.
- [ ] Selects a measurement method and individualized criterion with a rationale.
- [ ] Aligns the data tool with the target and documents relevant accommodations/conditions.

### 5. Data interpretation

- [ ] Checks data completeness, validity, and comparability.
- [ ] Verifies calculations against raw values.
- [ ] Describes level, trend, and variability without overstating conclusions.
- [ ] Identifies competing explanations and additional evidence needed.
- [ ] Does not treat a spreadsheet flag or small sample as proof of mastery or plateau.

### 6. Iterative design and testing

- [ ] Completes Plan → Refine → Build → Refine with educator direction at each stage.
- [ ] Builds one bounded artifact that serves the task.
- [ ] Tests the artifact with an appropriate scenario, learner input, or staff feedback.
- [ ] Records what was retained, changed, or rejected and why.
- [ ] Defines a next review point and evidence to collect.

### 7. Email triage (when applicable)

- [ ] Uses simulated messages or a specifically approved environment.
- [ ] Retains sender and subject as source context.
- [ ] Does not invent due dates or rank solely by sender title.
- [ ] Explains urgency using deadline, impact, and time sensitivity.
- [ ] Verifies original messages and takes no unapproved autonomous action.

### 8. Professional judgment and accessibility

- [ ] Independently verifies factual, technical, legal, and mathematical claims.
- [ ] Keeps evaluation, eligibility, placement, services, IEP decisions, and instructional judgment with qualified professionals and the team.
- [ ] Rejects unsupported or harmful model suggestions and can explain why.
- [ ] Produces materials usable with keyboard and screen-reader access; essential information is not conveyed only through images, color, or complex tables.

## Suggested interpretation

For the seven core areas excluding email triage, a participant may be considered ready for a supported pilot when every core area is rated 2 on the relevant observed behaviors, with no unresolved privacy or professional-judgment concern. If an item is N/O, gather evidence before making a readiness judgment. This is a local training suggestion, not a credential, employment evaluation, or substitute for district approval.

A participant may be highly competent without using an LLM for a particular task. Choosing not to use AI when the risk, privacy status, accessibility, or value is unfavorable is evidence of sound professional judgment.
