# Participant Pre- and Post-Assessment

This assessment measures practical judgment when using LLMs in special education planning, instruction, related services, and progress monitoring. It is not a test of prompt-engineering terminology or a measure of educator competence. Administer the pre-assessment before Session 1 and the parallel post-assessment after the Session 10 capstone. The applied post-assessment is not a Session 6 exit test. See the [Course Map and Assessment Plan](course-map-and-assessment-plan.md) for the complete schedule and how to interpret results alongside the separate AI-literacy assessment and capstone. Both forms use parallel tasks so participants can demonstrate growth without memorizing identical answers.

Use fictional information only. Do not enter student records into an AI tool for this assessment. Participants may complete the assessment with an approved LLM or by describing what they would ask the model to do.

## Participant directions

Complete the pre-assessment before Session 1. Complete the post-assessment only after Session 10. If a midpoint check is useful after Session 6, use a brief exit ticket rather than this post-assessment.

- Allow 20–25 minutes for each assessment.
- Answer independently before discussing with peers.
- Explain your reasoning. A defensible decision is more important than matching a preferred phrase.
- If a question lacks enough information, say what is missing rather than guessing.

## Pre-assessment

**1. Prompt boundaries**

A colleague asks: "Use AI to make a complete special education plan for this student." Write a short response describing how you would narrow the request. Include at least four boundaries or pieces of context you would specify.

**2. Facts, hypotheses, and unknowns**

A fictional student uses a tablet at school, has a visual impairment, and earns mostly Bs. A teacher says the student is independent with all digital materials.

List:
- Two facts explicitly provided.
- Two unknowns that should not be inferred.
- One hypothesis that could be investigated.
- One question you would ask the student.

**3. Goal measurement**

A draft target says: "The student will improve a targeted educational or related-service skill with 90% accuracy." Identify at least four missing elements needed to make this measurable and individualized.

**4. Data interpretation**

A learner completes 7 of 10 steps independently in one session and 9 of 10 steps with verbal prompts in another. Can these scores be combined as a single 80% independent-performance score? Explain what you would report and what information is needed.

**5. Possible plateau**

Performance appears unchanged across three data sessions. Name at least three competing explanations or data-quality questions you would investigate before changing instruction.

**6. Email triage**

A fictional message asks for an accessible document "before the lesson tomorrow" but does not state a clock time. What should an AI-generated action report say about the due date and urgency? What must the educator verify?

**7. Professional responsibility and privacy**

Name two safeguards you would apply before using an LLM with education-related information, and identify two decisions that must remain with educators or the IEP team.

## Post-assessment

**1. Prompt boundaries**

A colleague asks: "Analyze this learner's data and decide whether the IEP goal should change." Describe how you would reframe the task as bounded LLM assistance. Include context, constraints, output, and a stop condition.

**2. Facts, hypotheses, and unknowns**

A fictional student uses braille for some assignments, print for others, has a tablet, and completes familiar classroom routines successfully. A staff member says the student should use braille for every activity.

List:
- Two facts explicitly provided.
- Two unknowns that should not be inferred.
- One hypothesis that could be investigated.
- One question you would ask the student.

**3. Goal measurement**

A draft target says: "The learner will independently access a digital class handout using the agreed access method under classroom conditions." Identify at least four details needed to make the target measurable and individualized.

**4. Data interpretation**

A learner completes 6 of 8 defined steps independently in one session and 8 of 8 with a visual prompt in another. Explain why these results should be reported separately and what additional information is needed before describing progress.

**5. Possible plateau**

Performance varies little across four sessions, but two sessions used different materials and one had fewer opportunities. Name at least three checks you would complete before concluding that performance has plateaued.

**6. Email triage**

A fictional message from a colleague says: "Please review the route-practice note when you can." No due date or impact is stated. What should an AI-generated action report say about due date and urgency? What should the educator do next?

**7. Professional responsibility and privacy**

Describe two safeguards for using an LLM with education-related information and identify two decisions or professional judgments that must not be delegated to the model.

## Scoring guide

Score each item from 0 to 2. Maximum score: 14.

- **0 — Not yet demonstrated:** response is missing, unsafe, or treats model output as authoritative.
- **1 — Developing:** response identifies part of the issue but omits important constraints, verification, or professional judgment.
- **2 — Demonstrated:** response is accurate, bounded, practical, and identifies relevant unknowns and human review.

### Item-level scoring criteria

1. **Prompt boundaries:** identifies one task, relevant context, data limits, requested output, and stop condition; avoids asking the model to decide.
2. **Facts and unknowns:** distinguishes supplied facts from assumptions, names meaningful unknowns, and includes student perspective.
3. **Goal measurement:** identifies observable behavior, conditions, opportunities, independence/prompt definitions, measurement method, and individualized criterion.
4. **Data interpretation:** does not combine prompted and independent performance; identifies denominator, conditions, prompt definitions, and need to verify data.
5. **Plateau analysis:** considers data sufficiency and quality, task/material changes, opportunities, access/environment, instruction, prompt drift, and learner feedback.
6. **Email triage:** does not invent a date or deadline; gives a cautious urgency rationale and requires original-message verification.
7. **Privacy and responsibility:** uses approved systems and minimum necessary information; keeps evaluation, IEP, services, and instructional decisions with qualified professionals and the team.

## Facilitator answer notes

Accept equivalent answers when the reasoning is sound. Do not require participants to use identical wording.

- **Pre 1:** A strong response asks for one bounded planning task, supplies only relevant approved context, prohibits invented facts and diagnosis, requests unknowns and questions, specifies an accessible output, and stops before recommending goals or services.
- **Pre 2:** Facts include tablet use, visual impairment, and mostly B grades. Digital independence is not established by these facts. Ask which materials, tasks, settings, access methods, and support levels have been observed; ask the student about efficiency and preferences.
- **Pre 3:** Missing elements include the specific behavior/task, conditions, opportunities, operational definition, independence/prompt levels, data method, baseline, and individualized criterion.
- **Pre 4:** No. Report 7/10 independently and 9/10 with verbal prompts separately. Confirm the same steps, valid opportunities, prompt definitions, materials, and conditions.
- **Pre 5:** Consider insufficient observations, task variability, inconsistent materials, prompt changes, environment, fatigue, attendance, opportunity count, instructional fidelity, and measurement reliability.
- **Pre 6:** State that the message says "before the lesson tomorrow" and that the exact clock time is not stated. Urgency depends on lesson timing and preparation impact. Verify the original thread and local schedule; do not invent a time.
- **Post 1:** The model may summarize patterns, check calculations, identify missing data, and offer competing explanations. It must not decide whether an IEP goal changes. Request a verified description and stop before a recommendation is adopted.
- **Post 2:** Facts include use of braille for some assignments, print for others, tablet ownership/use, and familiar routine success. No single medium is established as appropriate for every activity. Ask the student about access, efficiency, fatigue, preferences, and task demands; consult the learning media assessment and team.
- **Post 3:** Specify the actual handout task, agreed access method, file format, device/settings, classroom conditions, number of opportunities, observable completion criteria, prompt levels, accuracy/efficiency measures, and baseline.
- **Post 4:** Report 6/8 independently and 8/8 with a visual prompt separately. Verify what the prompt was, whether steps and conditions were equivalent, and whether opportunities were valid.
- **Post 5:** Check whether data points are comparable, inspect the different materials, document opportunity counts, review prompt and accommodation consistency, check instruction and environment, and gather learner feedback.
- **Post 6:** Due date is not stated. Urgency is unclear until task impact and time sensitivity are known. Ask the sender or check context; do not rank by sender title alone.
- **Post 7:** Use district-approved tools, minimum necessary approved information, and privacy/retention safeguards. Do not delegate evaluation, eligibility, placement, services, IEP decisions, or professional instructional judgment.

## Interpreting results

Compare total scores and item-level patterns from pre to post. A higher score suggests stronger performance on these scenarios, not proof of classroom transfer. Compare this assessment only with its own parallel form; do not combine its 0–2 item scores with the AI-literacy assessment or the capstone's 0–3 rubric. Use the results to choose follow-up coaching. A score of 12–14 with no zero on privacy or professional responsibility may be used as a local course-completion suggestion, not a credential or district evaluation standard.

Record one practical behavior the participant will apply next and one support needed. Revisit the behavior during a later PLC or coaching conversation.
