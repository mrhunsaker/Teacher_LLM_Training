# Special Education Professional Learning: Practical LLM Use

This course teaches special education professionals to use large language models (LLMs) and Microsoft 365 Copilot as bounded, privacy-conscious productivity tools. It is designed for special education teachers, teachers of students with visual impairments (TVIs), teachers of deaf and hard-of-hearing students, school psychologists, speech-language pathologists (SLPs), occupational therapists (OTs), physical therapists (PTs), adapted physical education (APE) teachers, general education teachers, related-service providers, and classroom partners.

Participants choose one fictional case from the unified [Buendía-family case collection](multidisciplinary-case-studies.md) and use it throughout **Plan → Refine → Build → Refine**. All ten cases have equal standing and use the same learning pathway; no discipline is the default. The course supports instructional planning, related-service workflows, IEP-related preparation, observation and progress monitoring, data review, critical evaluation, and professional communication.

## Start here: the participant pathway

1. Read [Getting Started](getting-started.md).
2. Select one role-relevant case from the [Buendía-family case collection](multidisciplinary-case-studies.md). Use that case throughout the modules and workbook.
3. Complete the applied-course pre-assessment before Session 1.
4. Work through Sessions 1–6, using the [Participant Lesson Handouts](participant-lesson-handouts.md) as a working packet.
5. Complete the AI-literacy pre-assessment immediately before Session 7.
6. Work through Sessions 7–9 and complete the independent capstone in Session 10.
7. Complete both post-assessments after the capstone. Use the [Practical Competency Checklist](practical-competency-checklist.md) to identify strengths and coaching needs.

The [Course Map and Assessment Plan](course-map-and-assessment-plan.md) is the definitive source for the ten-session sequence, activities, assessment timing, participant evidence, and completion expectations.

## What participants will learn

By the end of the course, participants can:

- Write bounded prompts and keep AI within a defined task and professional scope.
- Separate facts, hypotheses, and unknowns; identify evidence and questions still needed.
- Draft or review role-appropriate educational targets and operational measures.
- Build aligned observation and progress-monitoring tools and verify spreadsheet calculations.
- Interpret data cautiously, including variability, prompt dependence, comparability, and competing explanations.
- Explain LLM limitations and identify hallucinations, omissions, false precision, and unsupported claims.
- Verify sources, calculations, accessibility, and privacy before using an AI-assisted artifact.
- Demonstrate Plan → Refine → Build → Refine and retain professional/team decision authority.

Choose one of the ten fictional cases in the [unified Buendía-family case collection](multidisciplinary-case-studies.md): Úrsula (vision and access), José Arcadio (deaf and hard-of-hearing education), Aureliano (school psychology), Amaranta (speech-language pathology), Rebeca (mathematics reasoning), Arcadio (reading), Fernanda (occupational therapy), Petra (physical therapy), Melquíades (adaptive physical education), and Pilar (general-education Tier 3 reading intervention). Each case includes known facts, unknowns, a bounded practice task, and professional boundaries.

## The ten-session sequence

### Sessions 1–6: Applied workflows

1. **Prompting:** Define a bounded task, role, context, constraints, output, and stop condition.
2. **Plan, Refine, and goal brainstorming:** Separate facts, hypotheses, and unknowns; identify student/team questions; draft role-appropriate target options for review.
3. **Data collection:** Define observable behaviors or outcomes, valid opportunities, prompt/support levels, and aligned data-collection tools.
4. **Excel progress monitoring:** Enter synthetic data, verify formulas and calculations, and interpret the graph in text.
5. **Data-based decisions:** Examine data quality, level, trend, variability, access conditions, and competing explanations.
6. **Copilot email triage:** Produce a human-reviewed action report from simulated email; verify original messages and dates.

### Sessions 7–10: AI literacy and independent performance

7. **AI literacy foundations:** Understand LLM generation, context, variability, and appropriate tool choice.
8. **Misconceptions and failure analysis:** Identify unsupported claims and correct flawed prompts and responses.
9. **Critical evaluation lab:** Verify claims, calculations, citations, accessibility, privacy, and task fit.
10. **Independent performance capstone:** Complete and explain an independently planned, built, refined, and verified artifact using a role-relevant fictional case.

Detailed agendas are in the [six-session applied facilitator guide](facilitator-practice-edition.md) and [four-session AI-literacy facilitator guide](ai-literacy-facilitator-guide.md). Together they cover all ten sessions. The [master course map](course-map-and-assessment-plan.md) resolves overall sequencing and assessment timing.

There are two parallel written assessments and one performance capstone:

- [Participant Pre- and Post-Assessment](participant-assessments.md): seven applied special-education scenarios; pre before Session 1 and post after Session 10.
- [AI Literacy and Misconception Assessment](ai-literacy-assessment.md): five AI-literacy scenarios; pre before Session 7 and post after Session 10.

Compare each written assessment only with its own parallel form and review item-level patterns. The [Practical Competency Checklist](practical-competency-checklist.md) uses an item-level 0–2 scale for formative observation and coaching. Do not combine its scores with the capstone's 0–3 rubric. None of these tools is a credential, employment evaluation, or proof of transfer to practice.

## The collaborative design cycle

The course uses **Plan → Refine → Build → Refine**, not one-shot prompt generation:

- **Plan:** Define the educational or related-service question, meaningful task, context, learner strengths, supplied facts, and unknowns.
- **Refine:** Use the LLM to surface assumptions, missing information, and questions. The professional narrows and approves the plan.
- **Build:** Draft one bounded artifact, such as a task analysis, observation form, communication-access check, or data summary.
- **Refine:** Test the artifact, review evidence and learner/staff feedback, and revise it.

The professional remains responsible for decisions within their role. IEP, evaluation, eligibility, placement, service, clinical, and other reserved decisions remain with qualified professionals and authorized teams.

## The role of the Expanded Core Curriculum

The Expanded Core Curriculum (ECC) is one specialized framework represented in this course, not its overall boundary. It supplements the general education curriculum with disability-specific skills that students with visual impairments may need to access information, participate meaningfully, and build independence. Its nine traditional areas are compensatory or functional academic skills (including communication modes), orientation and mobility, social interaction skills, independent living skills, recreation and leisure skills, career education, assistive technology, sensory efficiency, and self-determination.

The broader course also addresses communication access, academic instruction, school psychology problem-solving, related services, observation, progress monitoring, and cross-disciplinary collaboration. Instruction and services must be individualized; no learner is assumed to need every area.

## Implementation and safety requirements

- Use fictional or synthetic information for course practice. Removing a name alone does not establish de-identification.
- For real work, use only specifically district-approved tools and data workflows; follow privacy, retention, sharing, and reporting requirements.
- Do not infer functional vision, communication, behavior, cognition, literacy, independence, or educational need from a diagnosis, eligibility label, test score, acuity, grades, or device ownership alone.
- Distinguish independent from prompted performance. Define valid opportunities, operational measures, accommodations, communication modes, and task conditions.
- The greater-than-90%-across-three-sessions rule is a configurable training convention, not an IDEA or universal legal standard.
- LLM output and spreadsheet flags are aids, not authorities. Verify claims, calculations, and source material.
- Copilot email triage is advisory. Review original messages and deadlines; do not permit autonomous send, delete, archive, or commitments.
- Do not delegate diagnosis, evaluation, eligibility, placement, services, IEP-team decisions, or professional/clinical judgment to AI.
- Make accessibility checks part of the workflow. Text alternatives, semantic headings, keyboard access, and relevant screen-reader and communication workflows matter.

## Build and accessibility verification

The book is built with mdBook. From the repository root, run mdbook build and inspect the rendered output. Before release, verify internal links, heading hierarchy, keyboard navigation, and representative content with the screen readers and access technologies used by participants. A successful build does not by itself establish accessibility; record the actual test environment and results.
