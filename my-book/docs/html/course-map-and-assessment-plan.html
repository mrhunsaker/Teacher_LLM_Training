Special Education Professional Learning: Practical LLM Use

Course Map and Assessment Plan

This is the definitive self-study map for the course. It brings the six applied lessons and four AI-literacy modules into one ten-session sequence. Use it to plan your own study, follow the learning sequence, complete assessments, and check your course evidence.

At a glance

  • Audience: Special education professionals and classroom partners across disciplines.
  • Structure: 10 sessions, normally 75–90 minutes each.
  • Sequence: Sessions 1–6 build practical workflows; Sessions 7–10 build AI literacy, critical evaluation, and independent performance.
  • Case: Each participant selects one role-relevant fictional case from the unified Buendía-family case collection and carries that same case through the modules and workbook. All ten cases have equal standing; no discipline is the default.
  • Working method: Plan → Refine → Build → Refine.
  • Practice data: Use fictional cases and synthetic data. Use real education information only in a specifically district-approved environment and in accordance with policy.

Participant pathway

  1. Read Getting Started and select one role-relevant case from the unified Buendía-family case collection. Use that case throughout the course.
  2. Complete the applied-course pre-assessment before Session 1.
  3. Complete Sessions 1–6 and use Handouts 1–6 as the applied-course working packet. Use Handouts 7–9 during the AI-literacy and capstone sessions.
  4. Complete the AI-literacy pre-assessment immediately before Session 7.
  5. Complete Sessions 7–9.
  6. Complete the independent capstone in Session 10.
  7. Complete both post-assessments after the Session 10 capstone.
  8. Review the evidence portfolio and competency checklist; identify one next step for independent practice or optional consultation.

Sessions 1–6 are supported by the six-lesson self-study guide. Sessions 7–10 are supported by the AI-literacy self-study guide. The self-study guides provide detailed session-by-session directions; this map is the single source for overall sequence and assessment timing.

Assessment administration

Use the assessments at four points:

WhenAssessmentPurpose
Before Session 1Applied-course pre-assessmentEstablish initial practical judgment about prompts, evidence, measurement, data, email triage, privacy, and professional responsibility
Immediately before Session 7AI-literacy pre-assessmentEstablish initial understanding of LLM limits, verification, privacy, and when not to use AI
During Session 10Independent capstoneDemonstrate independent performance on a bounded fictional task; score with the capstone’s separate 0–3 rubric
After the Session 10 capstoneBoth parallel post-assessmentsCompare item-level learning patterns and identify next-step practice priorities

Do not administer the applied post-assessment at the end of Session 6. Session 6 completes the applied sequence, but the unified course post-assessment is given after Session 10 so participants can demonstrate learning across the full program. If a learner separately wants a midpoint check after Session 6, use an exit ticket or handout reflection; do not label it the course post-assessment.

How to interpret assessment evidence

  • Compare each written assessment only with its own parallel form: applied pre vs. applied post; AI-literacy pre vs. AI-literacy post.
  • Both written assessments score each response 0–2. These are knowledge/reasoning ratings, not observations of performance.
  • The competency checklist also uses 0–2, but rates individual observable behaviors during practice. Do not add its ratings to either written assessment.
  • The capstone uses a separate 0–3 domain rubric to score the independent performance demonstration. Do not convert or combine this scale with either 0–2 system.
  • Review item-level patterns and evidence, not only totals. Written assessments measure scenario-based reasoning; the capstone provides performance evidence under course conditions. Neither proves transfer to practice or professional certification.
  • Use the competency checklist for self-review or optional peer review during practice.
  • Scores are learning evidence, not employment evaluations or credentials.

Required participant evidence

By the end of Session 10, each participant should have:

  1. A role-relevant fictional case selection and a bounded task.
  2. A prompt sequence showing Plan → Refine → Build → Refine.
  3. A facts / hypotheses / unknowns record.
  4. A measurable target or clearly defined observation question, as appropriate to the participant’s role.
  5. An aligned task analysis, observation form, data tool, or other professional artifact.
  6. A synthetic-data interpretation with calculations checked against raw values.
  7. A critical review of a flawed AI response.
  8. A record of verified claims, sources, calculations, and accessibility/privacy checks.
  9. A capstone reflection stating what was retained, revised, rejected, and why.
  10. A next-step independent practice plan, including any optional consultation to seek.

Time and delivery options

The standard sequence is ten sessions of 75–90 minutes (approximately 12.5–15 hours of planned learning activities), plus independent reading and artifact completion. Complete each session in one sitting or divide it into shorter individual study blocks. Optional PLC discussion may supplement the work but is not required. Preserve the sequence and assessment points above; complete Sessions 7–10 as four distinct sessions.

Materials and prerequisites

  • The mdBook course and participant handouts.
  • One selected fictional case and the synthetic datasets.
  • Spreadsheet software for the Excel activity.
  • A district-approved LLM for demonstration, or the offline peer-review alternative.
  • Keyboard and screen-reader workflows used by participants.
  • Simulated email for the Copilot triage activity; personal or district inbox access is not required.

Participants should know the professional standards and scope-of-practice requirements relevant to their own roles.

Decision boundaries

AI may help brainstorm, organize, draft, check consistency, and identify questions. It does not diagnose, determine eligibility, interpret standardized assessments in place of qualified professionals, decide placement or services, make IEP-team decisions, or replace clinical, instructional, or school-psychology judgment. A decision not to use AI is an acceptable and sometimes preferred outcome.

Release validation

Complete the Accessibility and Release QA Checklist as part of course review. Record actual test results; a successful build does not establish accessibility.

Completion and transfer

Completion means the participant has completed the learning activities and submitted the expected evidence. It does not certify professional competence or prove transfer. A voluntary or locally approved follow-up 4–6 weeks later can examine whether the participant applies the workflow safely with fictional or approved information, verifies output, tests accessibility, and documents professional judgment.
