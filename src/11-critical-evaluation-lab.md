# Module 9 — Critical Evaluation Lab: Verify, Revise, or Reject

## Purpose

This lab develops a repeatable review routine for AI-assisted work. Participants practice checking factual claims, calculations, educational reasoning, accessibility, privacy, and alignment with the task before adopting any output.

## Learning outcomes

Participants will:

- Apply an evaluation sequence to AI-generated work.
- Check claims against appropriate authoritative sources or raw data.
- Identify missing context and unsupported assumptions.
- Revise or reject an output when verification fails.
- Document the educator's contribution and final decision.

## The six-step review sequence

1. **Scope:** Does the response answer only the task requested? Did it cross a stop condition?
2. **Evidence:** Which statements are supported by the prompt, observations, raw data, or verified sources?
3. **Accuracy:** Are calculations, citations, dates, product details, and policy claims correct?
4. **Fit:** Does the response fit this learner, task, setting, access needs, and instructional purpose?
5. **Accessibility and privacy:** Is the output usable with relevant access technologies, and was the information handled in an approved way?
6. **Decision:** Retain, revise, reject, or gather more evidence. Record why.

## Lab A: Goal draft audit

A fictional draft from an AI tool reads:

"Given classroom materials, the student will independently access digital assignments with 90% accuracy in three sessions."

Audit the draft. Identify at least six details that need clarification before the team could consider it. Examples include the actual task, agreed access method, materials/file format, device and settings, conditions, number of opportunities, operational definition of access, prompt levels, baseline, measure of accuracy, and rationale for the criterion.

Rewrite the sentence only after you have defined the measurement conditions. Do not assume that 90% is the right criterion for every learner or task.

## Lab B: Data and calculation audit

Use a fictional data record:

| Session | Independent correct | Valid opportunities | Prompted correct |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 6 | 8 | 2 |
| 2 | 7 | 10 | 1 |
| 3 | 5 | 8 | 3 |
| 4 | 8 | 10 | 0 |

Tasks:

1. Calculate the independent percentage for each session.
2. Keep prompted responses separate.
3. Check the denominator and ensure all opportunities are valid and comparable.
4. Describe the pattern without declaring mastery or plateau.
5. Identify what additional evidence would improve interpretation.

**Answer check:** independent percentages are 75%, 70%, 62.5%, and 80%. Do not combine prompted responses with independent responses. Four observations alone do not establish cause, mastery, or plateau.

## Lab C: Source verification

An AI-generated answer makes three claims:

- A particular software feature is available to every Microsoft 365 user.
- A specific privacy law requires all school staff to use one named AI product.
- A cited government guidance document says a specific percentage threshold is mandatory for all IEP goals.

For each claim:

1. Identify the exact factual proposition.
2. Find the official current product documentation, statute/regulation, or authoritative guidance.
3. Check date, jurisdiction, applicability, and exact wording.
4. Confirm that the source actually supports the claim.
5. Mark the claim verified, partially supported, outdated, unsupported, or false.
6. Remove or qualify claims that cannot be substantiated.

Do not use a second LLM response as the sole source of verification. The current official documentation, applicable law/regulation, district policy, and qualified professionals take precedence.

## Lab D: Accessibility audit

Inspect an AI-generated handout or data tool. Test:

- Heading hierarchy and meaningful link text.
- Logical reading order.
- Keyboard-only navigation and operation.
- Screen-reader announcement of controls, labels, and tables.
- Plain-language instructions and defined terms.
- Whether meaning depends only on color, position, or an image.
- Availability of a written interpretation for any chart.
- Compatibility with the access tools and workflow actually used by the intended participant or learner.

A document that looks clean visually is not necessarily accessible. Record the test environment and observed issues.

## Lab E: Decide whether to use AI

For each scenario, choose **use**, **use with safeguards**, or **do not use** and explain why.

1. Generate three alternative layouts for a fictional data sheet.
2. Paste a full student IEP into an unapproved public chatbot to summarize it.
3. Ask an approved AI assistant to rewrite a generic staff reminder in plain language.
4. Ask an LLM to decide whether a learner qualifies for a service.
5. Ask a model to identify missing fields in a synthetic CSV.
6. Ask a chatbot to explain a current software feature without checking official documentation.

There may be more than one defensible answer for some scenarios. The reasoning and safeguards matter.

## Exit ticket

Name one statement in an AI output that you would reject, one you would revise, and one you would retain. Explain the evidence and review action for each.

## Facilitator notes

Require participants to show their verification method, not merely state that they checked. For legal and product claims, direct participants to current official sources and district guidance. For data claims, return to raw values and definitions. For accessibility, require an actual test with appropriate technology rather than relying on visual inspection alone.
