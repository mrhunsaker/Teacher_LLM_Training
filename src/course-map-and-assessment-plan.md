# Course Map and Assessment Plan

This is the definitive delivery guide for the course. It brings the six applied lessons and four AI-literacy modules into one ten-session sequence. Use this page when planning delivery, orienting participants, administering assessments, or checking course completion.

## At a glance

- **Audience:** Special education professionals and classroom partners across disciplines.
- **Structure:** 10 sessions, normally 75–90 minutes each.
- **Sequence:** Sessions 1–6 build practical workflows; Sessions 7–10 build AI literacy, critical evaluation, and independent performance.
- **Case:** Each participant selects one role-relevant fictional case and carries it through the modules. The Aureliano Buendia vision/ECC case remains available alongside the multidisciplinary case studies.
- **Working method:** Plan → Refine → Build → Refine.
- **Practice data:** Use fictional cases and synthetic data. Use real education information only in a specifically district-approved environment and in accordance with policy.

## Participant pathway

1. Read [Getting Started](getting-started.md) and choose a case from [Aureliano Buendia — Vision/ECC](07-fictional-student-case.md) or the [Multidisciplinary Special Education Case Studies](multidisciplinary-case-studies.md).
2. Complete the applied-course pre-assessment before Session 1.
3. Complete Sessions 1–6 and use Handouts 1–6 as the applied-course working packet. Use Handouts 7–9 during the AI-literacy and capstone sessions.
4. Complete the AI-literacy pre-assessment immediately before Session 7.
5. Complete Sessions 7–9.
6. Complete the independent capstone in Session 10.
7. Complete both post-assessments after the Session 10 capstone.
8. Review the evidence portfolio and competency checklist; identify one next step for coaching or supported practice.

## Ten-session master map

| Session | Module and focus | Participant activity | Evidence or artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prompting and bounded tasks | Narrow a broad request; define role, task, context, constraints, output, and stop condition | Bounded prompt and revision |
| 2 | Plan, Refine, and IEP-related goal brainstorming | Use a selected case to separate facts, hypotheses, and unknowns; identify student/team questions; draft and review possible measurable targets within the participant's role | Planning brief and candidate target options |
| 3 | Data collection and operational measurement | Define observable behavior, valid opportunities, prompt/support levels, and aligned data-collection tools | Operational definition and aligned data tool |
| 4 | Excel progress monitoring | Enter synthetic data, verify formulas and calculations, graph performance, and describe the graph in text | Excel-ready tracker and verified data summary |
| 5 | Data-based decisions and refinement | Examine level, trend, variability, comparability, prompt dependence, and competing explanations | Evidence-based review and next-step plan |
| 6 | Copilot email triage and applied-course consolidation | Triage simulated email; verify original sources and dates; reflect on the six applied workflows | Advisory action report and applied-course reflection |
| 7 | AI literacy foundations | Compare generation with retrieval, databases, calculation, observation, and professional judgment | AI operation classification and limitation explanation |
| 8 | Misconceptions and failure analysis | Annotate a flawed response; identify unsupported claims; repair the prompt | E/I/U/X annotation and revised prompt |
| 9 | Critical evaluation lab | Verify claims, calculations, citations, accessibility, privacy, and task fit; decide retain/revise/reject/gather evidence | Completed critical-review record |
| 10 | Independent performance capstone | Independently plan, refine, build, verify, and explain work on a role-relevant fictional case | Capstone portfolio, 0–3 rubric, reflection |

Sessions 1–6 are supported by the [six-lesson facilitator guide](facilitator-practice-edition.md). Sessions 7–10 are supported by the [AI-literacy facilitator guide](ai-literacy-facilitator-guide.md). The guides provide the detailed agendas; this map is the single source for overall sequence and assessment timing.

## Assessment administration

Use the assessments at four points:

| When | Assessment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Before Session 1 | [Applied-course pre-assessment](participant-assessments.md#pre-assessment) | Establish initial practical judgment about prompts, evidence, measurement, data, email triage, privacy, and professional responsibility |
| Immediately before Session 7 | [AI-literacy pre-assessment](ai-literacy-assessment.md#pre-assessment) | Establish initial understanding of LLM limits, verification, privacy, and when not to use AI |
| During Session 10 | [Independent capstone](12-independent-performance-capstone.md) | Observe independent performance on a bounded fictional task; score with the capstone's separate 0–3 rubric |
| After the Session 10 capstone | Both parallel post-assessments | Compare item-level learning patterns and identify coaching needs |

**Do not administer the applied post-assessment at the end of Session 6.** Session 6 completes the applied sequence, but the unified course post-assessment is given after Session 10 so participants can demonstrate learning across the full program. If a facilitator separately wants a midpoint check after Session 6, use an exit ticket or handout reflection; do not label it the course post-assessment.

### How to interpret assessment evidence

- Compare each assessment with its own parallel form: applied pre vs. applied post; AI-literacy pre vs. AI-literacy post.
- Review item-level patterns, not only total scores.
- Written assessments measure scenario-based reasoning. They do not establish independent performance by themselves.
- The capstone provides performance evidence under course conditions, not proof of transfer to practice or professional certification.
- Use the competency checklist formatively during practice and coaching. Do not combine its 0–2 ratings with the capstone's 0–3 rubric; they measure related but different things.
- Scores are learning evidence, not employment evaluations or credentials.

## Required participant evidence

By the end of Session 10, each participant should have:

1. A role-relevant fictional case selection and a bounded task.
2. A prompt sequence showing Plan → Refine → Build → Refine.
3. A facts / hypotheses / unknowns record.
4. A measurable target or clearly defined observation question, as appropriate to the participant's role.
5. An aligned task analysis, observation form, data tool, or other professional artifact.
6. A synthetic-data interpretation with calculations checked against raw values.
7. A critical review of a flawed AI response.
8. A record of verified claims, sources, calculations, and accessibility/privacy checks.
9. A capstone reflection stating what was retained, revised, rejected, and why.
10. A next-step plan for supported practice or coaching.

## Time and delivery options

The default is ten sessions of 75–90 minutes (approximately 12.5–15 hours of facilitated instruction), plus independent reading and artifact completion. Facilitators may divide each session into shorter PLC segments or use a self-paced format, but should preserve the sequence and assessment points above. For the standard ten-session course, deliver Sessions 7–10 as four distinct sessions. If a local schedule uses shorter segments, preserve the order, all required activities, the independent capstone, and the assessment points.

## Materials and prerequisites

- The mdBook course and participant handouts.
- One selected fictional case and the synthetic datasets.
- Spreadsheet software for the Excel activity.
- A district-approved LLM for demonstration, or the offline peer-review alternative.
- Keyboard and screen-reader workflows used by participants.
- Simulated email for the Copilot triage activity; personal or district inbox access is not required.

No programming experience or ECC background is required for participants using non-vision cases. Participants should know the professional standards and scope-of-practice requirements relevant to their own roles.

## Decision boundaries

AI may help brainstorm, organize, draft, check consistency, and identify questions. It does not diagnose, determine eligibility, interpret standardized assessments in place of qualified professionals, decide placement or services, make IEP-team decisions, or replace clinical, instructional, or school-psychology judgment. A decision not to use AI is an acceptable and sometimes preferred outcome.

## Release validation

Before publishing or distributing the course, run the mdBook build and complete the [Accessibility and Release QA Checklist](accessibility-and-release-checklist.md). Build success, internal-link review, keyboard testing, and screen-reader testing are separate checks; record the actual results rather than assuming one proves the others.

## Completion and transfer

Completion means the participant has completed the learning activities and submitted the expected evidence. It does not certify professional competence or prove transfer. A voluntary or locally approved follow-up 4–6 weeks later can examine whether the participant applies the workflow safely with fictional or approved information, verifies output, tests accessibility, and documents professional judgment.
