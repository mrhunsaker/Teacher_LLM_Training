# Facilitator and Practice Edition

This chapter provides the detailed facilitator plans for Sessions 1–6 of the ten-session professional-learning course. Sessions 7–10 are detailed in the [AI-literacy facilitator guide](ai-literacy-facilitator-guide.md); the [Course Map and Assessment Plan](course-map-and-assessment-plan.md) is the definitive source for the full sequence and assessment timing. It is designed for special education professionals—including special education and general education teachers, TVIs, teachers of deaf and hard-of-hearing students, school psychologists, speech-language pathologists, and related-service colleagues—with varied AI experience. Participants select a fictional case aligned with their role and scope of practice. No programming is required.

## Course purpose

Participants learn to use an LLM as a bounded drafting, questioning, and analysis partner. The model does not replace student assessment, educator judgment, IEP-team decisions, or professional responsibility.

## Recommended delivery

Sessions 1–6 are 75–90 minutes each. Deliver them as six PLC meetings, a two-day workshop, or self-paced study. They are followed by Sessions 7–10; do not describe the six applied lessons alone as the complete course. Use fictional profiles and synthetic data for all practice.

## Course outcomes

Participants will be able to:

1. Construct a bounded prompt with role, task, context, constraints, output, and stop condition.
2. Separate known facts, hypotheses, and unknowns before planning instruction.
3. Draft and professionally review an individualized educational or related-service target appropriate to the selected case and professional role.
4. Build observable task analyses and aligned data tools for the service provider and classroom partner or second observer, where appropriate.
5. Verify a model's interpretation against raw data, conditions, and student perspective.
6. Produce a human-reviewed action report from simulated email.
7. Document iterative Plan → Refine → Build → Refine work.

## Materials

- Existing course chapters and final special education workbook.
- District-approved LLM access, or the offline peer-review alternative.
- Fictional student cases and synthetic datasets in this edition.
- Spreadsheet software for data practice.
- Keyboard and screen-reader access technology used by participants.

Do not require participants to connect personal or district email accounts. The email lab uses simulated messages.

## Lesson 1: Bounded prompting

**Objectives:** Identify task, context, constraints, output, and stop condition; revise a broad prompt; detect unsupported claims.

**Preparation:** Read Module 1 and the Prompt Library. Prepare one generic special education or related-service task.

**75–90 minute agenda:**
- 0–10: Identify current AI use and one risk.
- 10–25: Demonstrate a broad prompt and bounded revision.
- 25–45: Pairs revise weak prompts: "Make a complete special education plan"; "Write a goal"; "Analyze this data"; "Choose an intervention."
- 45–65: Run or simulate one prompt; annotate facts, assumptions, and unknowns.
- 65–80: Peer review using the prompt checklist.
- 80–90: Exit ticket: name one boundary to add to future prompts.

**Artifact:** One bounded prompt and a note identifying decisions the model must not make.

**Evidence:** The prompt has one task, relevant context, explicit limits, an accessible output format, and a stop condition.

## Lesson 2: Plan, Refine, and goal brainstorming

**Objectives:** Separate facts, hypotheses, and unknowns; use an LLM to surface questions; narrow an educational or service problem to an observable task; brainstorm and professionally review candidate target language within the participant's role.

**Preparation:** Use Aureliano Buendia's vision case or select a relevant fictional case from the multidisciplinary case-study chapter.

**Agenda:**
- 0–10: Review the educator-led design cycle.
- 10–25: Demonstrate a facts / hypotheses / unknowns analysis.
- 25–45: Participants write a Plan prompt.
- 45–65: Participants write a Refine prompt requesting missing observations and competing explanations.
- 65–75: Select one meaningful task and define needed evidence.
- 75–85: Draft up to three candidate target statements or outcome descriptions; mark all missing information and decisions reserved to the educator/team.
- 85–90: Exit ticket: what question remains and who can answer it?

**Artifact:** Planning brief and role-appropriate candidate target options, with missing information and team decisions clearly marked.

**Evidence:** No inference about functional vision, literacy medium, motivation, or independence is made from diagnosis, acuity, grades, or device ownership alone.

## Lesson 3: Data collection and operational measurement

**Objectives:** Translate a selected target or service outcome into an operational definition; specify valid opportunities, prompt/support levels, conditions, and aligned data fields.

**Preparation:** Read Module 3 and use the target options and fictional baseline from Lesson 2.

**Agenda:**
- 0–15: Compare vague and operationally defined measures.
- 15–30: Define behavior/outcome, conditions, valid opportunities, and scoring rules.
- 30–50: Build a data-collection tool matched to the selected discipline and task.
- 50–70: Peer review the tool for alignment, usability, and consistent scoring.
- 70–85: Discuss why measures and criteria must be individualized and construct-valid.
- 85–90: Exit ticket: what would make the measure invalid in another setting?

**Artifact:** Operational definition, aligned data-collection tool, and professional review notes.

**Evidence:** The measure fits the selected skill or outcome, distinguishes independent and supported performance where relevant, and records conditions. Greater than 90% across three sessions is a configurable training convention, not a universal standard.

## Lesson 4: Excel progress monitoring

**Objectives:** Enter aligned session data in a spreadsheet; verify formulas and calculations; interpret a graph in text; identify data-quality limitations.

**Preparation:** Read Module 4. Open one synthetic dataset and the data tool built in Lesson 3.

**Agenda:**
- 0–15: Review the operational definition and data fields from Lesson 3.
- 15–35: Enter synthetic session data and verify denominators and prompt levels.
- 35–55: Calculate at least two synthetic data rows manually and check spreadsheet formulas.
- 55–70: Describe the graph and values in text.
- 70–85: Identify a data-quality issue and its impact.
- 85–90: Exit ticket: what else is needed before comparing sessions?

**Artifact:** Completed spreadsheet tracker and written data summary with verification notes.

**Evidence:** The plan documents valid opportunities, prompt levels, accommodations, conditions, and missing data.

## Lesson 5: Interpret and refine

**Objectives:** Separate description from explanation; identify trend and variability; generate a testable next step.

**Preparation:** Read Module 5 and select one of the three datasets.

**Agenda:**
- 0–15: Demonstrate cautious data description.
- 15–35: Groups inspect improving, variable, or possible-plateau data.
- 35–50: Ask the model for competing explanations.
- 50–65: Verify arithmetic and claims against raw rows.
- 65–80: Select one testable next step and evidence to review.
- 80–90: Exit ticket: what evidence would disconfirm your explanation?

**Artifact:** Verified interpretation, limitations, possible explanations, and review plan.

**Evidence:** No causal claim is made from a small dataset; instruction is not changed solely because a model recommends it.

## Lesson 6: Copilot workflow and applied-course consolidation

**Objectives:** Extract tasks and dates from simulated email; assign transparent urgency; verify original sources; reflect on the applied workflows. The independent capstone occurs in Session 10.

**Preparation:** Read Module 6 and the simulated email lab.

**Agenda:**
- 0–15: Demonstrate email triage using fictional messages.
- 15–35: Participants produce an action report and verify sources.
- 35–45: Discuss uncertainty, urgency, and importance.
- 45–75: Complete the simulated email triage lab and verify each action against the original message.
- 75–85: Applied-course reflection: identify one workflow to carry into the AI-literacy phase and one boundary to retain.
- 85–90: Exit ticket: what district approval is needed before real-world use?

**Artifact:** Verified advisory action report and applied-course reflection.

**Evidence:** The report labels unknown dates, explains urgency, retains source context, and takes no autonomous email action. The independent capstone and final portfolio are completed in Session 10, not Session 6.

## Instructor notes and expected answers

- **Facts** are explicitly provided. **Unknowns** are not supplied. **Hypotheses** are plausible explanations requiring investigation.
- Acuity, diagnosis, and grades do not establish functional vision, access efficiency, literacy medium, or instructional need.
- A target should define observable behavior, conditions, independence or prompt level, measurement, and an individualized criterion.
- A possible plateau describes a pattern, not its cause. Consider too few observations, inconsistent opportunities, task difficulty, prompt drift, environment, fatigue, attendance, instructional fidelity, and measurement reliability.
- Sender role is one cue in email triage; urgency should depend on verified deadline, impact, and time sensitivity.
- When the model invents a fact, ask which input supports the claim and how the workflow should prevent it.

## Competency feedback

Use the item-level [Practical Competency Checklist](practical-competency-checklist.md) for formative observation, peer review, and coaching. Each behavior is rated 0, 1, 2, or N/O with an evidence note. Do not create a second competency scale from this facilitator guide. The independent capstone in Session 10 uses its own 0–3 domain rubric; keep that score separate from the checklist and written assessments.

## Final evidence portfolio

Participants submit or present:

1. One bounded prompt and revision.
2. One planning brief separating facts, hypotheses, and unknowns.
3. One draft target and professional review notes.
4. One task analysis or instructional artifact.
5. One data-collection plan defining independent and prompted performance.
6. One synthetic data interpretation with verified calculations.
7. One documented instructional refinement and review plan.
8. One simulated email action report with source verification.
9. A reflection describing a model error, limitation, or uncertainty and how it was handled.

Use the item-level [Practical Competency Checklist](practical-competency-checklist.md) for formative coaching. The separate 0–3 capstone rubric is used in Session 10. Do not combine the two scoring systems. This is a course-design suggestion, not a professional credential or district evaluation standard.
