# Unit 1: Applied workflows

Unit 1 develops a practical, human-directed workflow for using large language models (LLMs) in special education and related-service work. Across Sessions 1–6, you will learn to define bounded tasks, refine prompts, brainstorm role-appropriate targets, build aligned data tools, review progress data, and triage simulated email without delegating professional decisions to AI.

Work through the materials below in order. Use one fictional case from the [Unified Buendía-Family Special Education Case Studies](multidisciplinary-case-studies.md) throughout the unit, and retain the artifacts you create for the final workbook.

## Unit materials

### [1.2 AI Skills Self-Directed Practice Guide — Sessions 1–6](self-directed-practice-guide.md)

The guide provides the independent-learning sequence, estimated session times, reading assignments, practice steps, evidence to save, and self-check criteria for the first six sessions. It explains how to assemble a portfolio of prompts, planning notes, measurement tools, data interpretations, and a verified email action report.

### [1.3 Hands-on Practice Labs](practice-labs.md)

Use the five structured labs to practice bounded prompting, goal development and measurement, data analysis, simulated Copilot email triage, and the Plan → Refine → Build → Refine cycle. Each lab includes fictional scenarios, participant steps, and evidence to retain.

### [1.4 Lesson 1 — Prompting an LLM with Clear Boundaries](01-prompting.md)

Learn to construct prompts with a defined role, one task, relevant context, explicit constraints, an output format, and a stop condition. Practice iterative prompt design and checking model output for invented facts, scope creep, and unsupported assumptions.

### [1.5 Lesson 2 — IEP-Related Goal Brainstorming](02-iep-goals.md)

Prepare a concise fictional learner snapshot and ask an LLM to brainstorm candidate educational or service targets within your professional role. Evaluate whether each option is observable, measurable, individualized, and connected to baseline evidence; leave final decisions to qualified professionals and the IEP team.

### [1.6 Lesson 3 — Building Aligned Data-Collection Tools](03-data-collection.md)

Translate a target skill or service outcome into observable steps and valid opportunities. Build data-collection tools that align with the task and record relevant conditions, prompts, supports, and generalization so results can be interpreted consistently.

### [1.7 Lesson 4 — Excel Progress Monitoring and Criterion Flags](04-excel-monitoring.md)

Use synthetic data in a spreadsheet to calculate and display progress. Check formulas, denominators, and prompt levels; interpret criterion flags as review cues rather than automatic instructional decisions.

### [1.8 Lesson 5 — Interpreting Progress, Growth, and Possible Plateaus](05-data-based-decisions.md)

Describe level, trend, and variability in progress-monitoring data without overstating what a small dataset establishes. Compare possible explanations against raw records and conditions, identify missing evidence, and plan a testable next step.

### [1.9 Lesson 6 — Microsoft Copilot Morning Email Triage](06-copilot-email.md)

Practice extracting actions and deadlines from simulated messages and producing an advisory report with source verification. Preserve uncertainty, account for superseded requests, and keep all email actions under human control.

### [1.10 Final Special Education Capstone Workbook](08-final-workbook.md)

Consolidate the unit's work in a text-based workbook. Organize the prompts, planning brief, candidate targets, data tools, verified interpretations, and simulated email report that will support your independent performance work.

## Unit 1 checkpoint

Before moving to Unit 2, review your evidence portfolio and confirm that you can define a bounded task, distinguish facts from hypotheses and unknowns, build a task-aligned measurement tool, verify calculations and interpretations, and explain which decisions remain with the responsible professional or team.
