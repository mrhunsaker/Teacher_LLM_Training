Special Education Professional Learning: Practical LLM Use

Final Unit Workbook: Plan, Refine, Build, Refine

Purpose

Use this workbook to plan, develop, test, and revise an individualized educational or related-service workflow for a student receiving special education services. It is designed for special education professionals, including teachers, TVIs, teachers of deaf and hard-of-hearing students, school psychologists, speech-language pathologists, related-service providers, classroom teachers, families, and other relevant team members. Select a case and target that fit your role and scope of practice.

Choose one role-relevant case from the unified Buendía-family case collection. The collection includes six equal options and a worked example of the shared process; no discipline is the default. No case profile is a template for a real student. Replace every case detail with verified information and professional judgment appropriate to the learner and your role.

This is a working document. Complete it in stages; do not ask an LLM to fill it all out in one request. Use the prompts at each stage, review the response, make professional edits, and decide whether to proceed.

How to use the workbook

  1. Work with fictional or district-approved de-identified information only when using an LLM.
  2. Complete the professional planning sections before asking the model to help.
  3. Use one bounded prompt at a time. Tell the model what it must not do and where to stop.
  4. Treat model output as a draft. Correct errors, reject unsuitable suggestions, and document decisions.
  5. Test tools with the student and team. Revise based on observations, student feedback, and data.
  6. Keep IEP, assessment, eligibility, service, placement, and safety decisions with the qualified professionals and the appropriate team.

Privacy reminder: Removing a name is not always sufficient to de-identify a record. Do not include initials, birth dates, student numbers, school or district identifiers, addresses, photographs, exact schedules, or unusual combinations of details that could identify a student. Follow district policy and use only approved AI environments for education records.

Copy and adapt this prompt only after you have prepared a de-identified summary.

You are a collaborative planning assistant for a special education professional. My role and scope of practice are: [state role]. I am in the PLAN stage, not the goal-writing or lesson-building stage.

Task: Organize the student information below into strengths, relevant verified facts, direct observations, and questions that still need investigation.

Context: [Paste only the minimum district-approved de-identified summary.]

Boundaries: Do not infer functional vision, communication, literacy, behavior, cognition, eligibility, services, placement, or a diagnosis. 6C. Spreadsheet and graph review

If using the course Excel tracker:

  • Check that the denominator reflects actual opportunities.
  • Confirm that blank cells are not treated as zero performance.
  • Verify that the formula matches the team’s defined measure.
  • Inspect the graph for level, trend, variability, and unusual points.
  • Record prompt levels and conditions alongside the numeric score.
  • Confirm that the spreadsheet’s criterion rule matches the current team-approved criterion.

The course example of greater than 90% across at least three separate sessions is only a configurable training convention. It is not a universal IEP rule, legal standard, or automatic definition of mastery. Do not apply it unless it is appropriate for the target and explicitly selected by the team.

6D. LLM data-review prompt

Help me review this de-identified progress-monitoring summary. Do not decide mastery, change the IEP, or diagnose a plateau.

First check data sufficiency and comparability. Identify missing sessions, inconsistent opportunities, changes in prompt level, changes in task difficulty, access or environmental differences, and possible implementation-fidelity issues.

Then summarize only patterns supported by the data: level, direction of change, variability, independence, and generalization across people or settings. Distinguish observations from hypotheses. If the data are insufficient, say so and identify what additional information would help.

Do not invent values or recommend reducing accommodations, services, communication supports, access methods, or other supports.

[Paste only an appropriately de-identified summary.]

7C. Refine-stage LLM prompt

Act as a critical planning partner. Compare the original plan, the implementation notes, student feedback, and the de-identified data summary below.

Identify: (1) what appears to be working; (2) what is not yet supported by evidence; (3) inconsistencies or missing data; (4) possible revisions to the tool or teaching routine; and (5) questions that require professional or team judgment.

Do not decide whether the student has mastered the goal, diagnose a plateau, recommend changing eligibility or services, or remove accommodations. Offer no more than three revision options and explain what evidence would support each. Keep facts, hypotheses, and recommendations in separate sections.
