Special Education Professional Learning: Practical LLM Use

Participant Lesson Handouts

Use this packet as a working record across the ten-session course. Select one role-relevant case from the unified Buendía-family case collection and carry it through the activities. All ten cases are equal options. Adapt each task to your professional role and scope of practice.

These handouts are text-first and can be completed in a copy, printed, or used with a screen reader. Use fictional or district-approved information only. Do not enter identifiable student or staff information into an unapproved system.

Handout 1 — Bounded prompt planner

Purpose: Define one task, its boundaries, the output, and the point where the model must stop.

Plan the request

Task and purpose

Write one specific task. Avoid broad requests such as “make a complete plan.”

Intended audience

Name who will use the output:

Relevant context that may be shared

Include only the minimum facts needed for this task. Identify the approved source or fictional case.

Constraints and prohibited actions

List assumptions, decisions, or adjacent tasks the model must not take on.

Required output

Specify format, length, headings, reading level, and accessibility requirements.

Stop condition

State exactly where the model must stop and ask before continuing.

Draft and test

Prompt draft

Quality check

  • One task only.
  • Facts are separated from hypotheses and unknowns.
  • Only fictional or approved information is included.
  • The model is not asked to diagnose, evaluate, or make a team decision.
  • Output requirements include accessible text and a usable structure.
  • Claims requiring verification are identified.
  • A clear stop condition is included.

Revision after testing

What did you change? Why was the change needed?

Handout 3 — Goal and measurement planner

Purpose: Make the target observable and the measurement interpretable. Use this handout for an educational goal, service outcome, or role-appropriate observation target.

Define the target

Target skill and meaningful outcome

Observable behavior

What will the learner do that another observer could identify?

Conditions and materials

Where, when, with what materials, access tools, communication supports, and accommodations?

Define the measurement

Valid opportunity and denominator

What counts as an opportunity? What makes an opportunity invalid or non-comparable?

Independent and prompted performance

Define independence and each relevant prompt or assistance level. Do not combine prompted and independent results.

Measurement method

Select and justify: count, percentage, duration, latency, rubric, work sample, or another measure.

Baseline evidence

Record sessions, numerators and denominators, conditions, and relevant variation.

Candidate criterion and rationale

Explain why the criterion is individualized and educationally meaningful.

Questions requiring IEP or team review

Important: A threshold such as greater than 90% across three sessions is a training example, not a universal legal, clinical, or IEP requirement.

Handout 4 — Build and test an instructional or service artifact

Purpose: Create one small, usable artifact and test it before relying on it.

Specify the artifact

Artifact selected

Examples: task analysis, checklist, data sheet, practice sequence, staff note, communication-access protocol, or meeting-question list.

Intended user and task

Required information, fields, or steps

Define each field or step in plain language.

Accessibility requirements

Consider headings, reading order, keyboard use, screen-reader labels, plain language, and nonvisual alternatives.

Record the test

Test scenario, participant, and technology

What happened?

Record observable friction, errors, or barriers—not assumptions about why they occurred.

Learner or staff feedback

Record the source and distinguish direct feedback from your interpretation.

One revision to test next

Describe the smallest useful change.

Handout 5 — Data review and refinement record

Purpose: Describe what the data support, what remains unknown, and what should be checked next.

Identify the evidence

Target and operational definition

Data source and sessions reviewed

Identify the synthetic dataset or approved source. State the date range or session identifiers.

Independent performance

Report numerator and denominator for each session. Keep sessions separate.

Prompted performance

Report separately, using the defined prompt levels.

Conditions, accommodations, materials, and changes

Interpret cautiously

What the data show

Describe level, trend, variability, and data sufficiency. Verify calculations against raw values.

What the data do not establish

State limits. Avoid causal claims or unsupported conclusions about mastery or plateau.

Competing explanations

Evidence that would distinguish the explanations

One small next step and review point

State what will be tested, who is responsible, and what evidence will be collected.

Handout 7 — AI-output review: evidence, inference, unknown, error

Purpose: Classify important statements in an AI response before relying on them.

Use the classification key

  • E — Evidence: Explicitly present in the prompt or a verified source.
  • I — Inference: A possible interpretation that requires review.
  • U — Unknown: Not established by available information.
  • X — Unsupported or error: Invented, contradicted, or stated with unjustified certainty.

Review one statement

Statement from the AI response

Use a fictional practice statement.

My label: E / I / U / X

Reason for the label

Identify the supporting evidence or explain what is missing or contradicted.

What I would verify or ask next

Name the source, observation, calculation, or person to consult.

Disposition

  • Retain
  • Revise
  • Reject
  • Gather more evidence

Handout 8 — Six-step critical review record

Purpose: Apply a consistent review before accepting or sharing an AI-assisted artifact.

Review the response

  1. Scope: Did the response stay within the task and stop condition?
  2. Evidence: Which statements are supported by supplied facts or verified sources?
  3. Accuracy: Did I check calculations, dates, citations, product details, and policy claims?
  4. Fit: Does it fit the learner, task, setting, access needs, and purpose?
  5. Accessibility and privacy: Is the artifact usable, and was information handled appropriately?
  6. Decision: What will I retain, revise, reject, or investigate further?

Record the review

One claim verified

Claim and source, raw data, or calculation checked.

One issue corrected or rejected

Describe the issue and rationale.

Accessibility test performed or still needed

Identify technology, test conditions, and result. If not tested, state the next test.
