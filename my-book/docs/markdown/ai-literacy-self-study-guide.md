# AI Literacy Self-Study Guide — Sessions 7–10

This guide supports independent completion of the final four sessions in the ten-session course. It assumes you have completed Sessions 1–6 and continue using the same fictional case. Work through the sequence on your own; discussion with colleagues is optional, not required. The [Self-Directed Practice Guide](self-directed-practice-guide.md) covers Sessions 1–6. The [Course Map and Assessment Plan](course-map-and-assessment-plan.md) is the definitive source for sequence and assessment timing.

All ten fictional cases have equal standing; no discipline is the default. Use the case selected at the start of the course. Do not introduce real student records into an unapproved tool.

## How to complete this phase

For each session, read the linked module, complete the practice steps, save the listed evidence, and use the self-check questions before proceeding. If you cannot verify a claim or safely complete a task, record the limitation and stop rather than guessing.

## Session 7 — AI literacy foundations

**Estimated time:** 75–90 minutes.

**Before you begin:** Complete the AI-literacy pre-assessment in [AI Literacy Assessment](ai-literacy-assessment.md), then read [Module 7](09-ai-literacy-foundations.md).

**Work through:**
1. Explain in your own words how an LLM generates a response from input and context.
2. Distinguish generation from retrieval, database lookup, calculation, observation, and professional judgment.
3. Test one bounded prompt using fictional information, or walk through the example without using a live model.
4. Identify what the model cannot know from the supplied prompt.
5. List one task where another tool or a human process is more appropriate.

**Save:** A short explanation of one model limitation and a task-selection note.

**Self-check:** Can you explain why fluent output is not verification? Can you name a task where AI should not be used and explain why?

## Session 8 — Misconceptions and failure analysis

**Estimated time:** 75–90 minutes.

**Read:** [Module 8](10-misconceptions-and-failure-analysis.md).

**Work through:**
1. Review the misconceptions identified in your pre-assessment.
2. Apply the E/I/U/X annotation method to the deliberately flawed response in the module.
3. Mark evidence, inference, unknowns, and unsupported or unsafe claims.
4. Identify missing evidence and rewrite the prompt so the model identifies unknowns rather than making a decision.
5. Compare your annotations with the answer notes included in the assessment and module.

**Save:** The annotated response, missing-evidence list, and revised bounded prompt.

**Self-check:** Does each factual claim have support in the supplied case? Have you separated a plausible explanation from an established fact? Have you removed any diagnosis, eligibility, placement, service, or IEP-team decision from the model's task?

## Session 9 — Critical evaluation lab

**Estimated time:** 75–90 minutes.

**Read:** [Module 9](11-critical-evaluation-lab.md).

**Work through:**
1. Apply the six-step review sequence to the sample goal, service-outcome, or observation-target draft.
2. Recalculate the synthetic data and compare your results with the spreadsheet.
3. Verify one product, policy, or legal claim against a current authoritative source.
4. Test the generated artifact's accessibility using the relevant keyboard, screen-reader, or other access technology where available. If you cannot perform a test, record it as not tested.
5. Decide whether to retain, revise, reject, or gather more evidence for each part of the output.

**Save:** A completed review record with at least one verified claim, one corrected issue, and an accessibility test plan or result.

**Self-check:** Have you verified the exact claim rather than merely confirming that a cited source exists? Are calculations reproducible? Is the artifact usable by its intended audience? Are privacy and task-fit concerns addressed?

## Session 10 — Independent performance capstone

**Estimated time:** 90 minutes, plus post-assessments after the capstone.

**Read:** [Module 10](12-independent-performance-capstone.md) and the [Practical Competency Checklist](practical-competency-checklist.md).

**Work through:**
1. Select a bounded task from your chosen fictional case.
2. Plan the task and separate known facts, hypotheses, and unknowns.
3. Refine the plan by identifying missing evidence, alternative explanations, and professional boundaries.
4. Build one small artifact.
5. Verify claims, calculations, privacy, accessibility, and alignment with the task.
6. Refine the artifact and document what you retained, revised, rejected, and still need to verify.
7. Explain why the artifact is appropriate—or why you chose not to use AI.

**Save:** The capstone artifact, prompt/revision history, verification record, completed rubric, and reflection.

**Self-check:** Can another professional understand the task, evidence, limits, and revision decisions? Does the artifact avoid making a reserved professional or team decision? Is every claim either verified, clearly marked as uncertain, or removed?

## Post-assessments

After completing the capstone, complete both post-assessments in a separate block (35–45 minutes total):

- Applied-course post-assessment: 20–25 minutes.
- AI-literacy post-assessment: 15–20 minutes.

Do not compress both assessments into the final ten minutes of the capstone. The [Course Map and Assessment Plan](course-map-and-assessment-plan.md) and each assessment's instructions specify that both post-assessments occur after the capstone.

## Independent learning principles

- Say “we do not know” rather than filling gaps with guesses.
- Require evidence and verification methods, not merely confidence.
- Correct unsafe claims and document why they are unsafe.
- Keep examples fictional or use only specifically approved tools and data.
- Do not assume one model description or product workflow applies to every AI system.
- Treat choosing not to use AI as a sound professional judgment when appropriate.
- Do not treat assessment scores as credentials or employment evaluations.

## If your self-review identifies a gap

If you accepted an unsupported student-specific claim, return to the facts / hypotheses / unknowns method. If you mixed prompted and independent data, revisit the data definitions and raw record. If you relied on a citation-like string, verify the source and exact claim. If you overlooked accessibility, perform an actual keyboard/screen-reader test or record that the test remains outstanding. Reassess with a parallel fictional scenario after additional practice.

## Course completion evidence

A complete evidence portfolio should contain:

- original and revised bounded prompts,
- participant handouts,
- pre/post assessment responses,
- annotated flawed AI output,
- one artifact and its revision record,
- data verification record,
- accessibility/privacy review,
- independent capstone rubric,
- next-step practice plan.

The portfolio documents learning in the course. It does not independently certify professional competence or replace district approval and ongoing supervision.
